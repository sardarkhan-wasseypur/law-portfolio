Judicial Clarifications

Frequently Asked Criminal Law Questions

1. How quickly can an Anticipatory Bail application be listed before the Delhi High Court or Sessions Court? expand_more
Before the Sessions Court, upon filing in the morning registry (before 12:30 PM), the bail application is typically numbered and placed before the Special/Sessions Judge either on the same afternoon or the following morning. Before the Delhi High Court, in urgent apprehension situations, chamber counsel can file an urgent application with a mentioning slip before the Hon'ble Chief Justice for next-day or even same-day vacation listing.
2. What should I do immediately upon receiving a Police Notice under Section 41A CrPC? expand_more
Never ignore a Section 41A CrPC (or Section 35 BNSS) notice. Non-appearance grants the investigating officer statutory discretion to arrest you. Immediately instruct legal counsel to draft a structured written response, appear on the scheduled date accompanied by an advocate or formal submission, and verify compliance with Supreme Court directives in Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar.
3. Can an FIR be quashed if a settlement has been reached between private parties? expand_more
Yes, under Section 482 CrPC (Section 528 BNSS), the Delhi High Court holds inherent jurisdiction to quash FIRs and resultant proceedings in matters that are predominantly civil, matrimonial, commercial, or personal in nature, provided both parties file a settlement deed, joint affidavits, and record their statements before the Court or Registrar. Non-compoundable heinous offenses against the State cannot be quashed merely on compromise.
4. What happens if a regular bail application is rejected by the District Sessions Judge? expand_more
Upon rejection by the Court of Session, an immediate petition under Section 439 CrPC is filed before the Delhi High Court. Our chambers obtains the certified rejection order, analyzes the bench's findings, highlights mitigating grounds—such as incarceration period, completion of investigation, chargesheet status, parity with co-accused—and seeks urgent listing before the High Court roster bench.
5. Does Advocate Shubham Singh accept urgent late-night remand or holiday duty representation? expand_more
Yes. Under Indian criminal procedure, an arrested person must be produced before a Judicial Magistrate within 24 hours, which frequently occurs at the residential office of the Duty Magistrate during night hours, weekends, or court holidays. Our chambers maintains an active duty counsel protocol to represent clients during off-hour remand productions.
6. How are Non-Bailable Warrants (NBWs) recalled or stayed in Delhi courts? expand_more
NBWs issued due to non-appearance can be addressed by moving an immediate application for cancellation/recall under Section 70(2) CrPC before the issuing magistrate, supported by bona fide grounds such as medical incapacity or lack of service. If execution is imminent, an emergency criminal revision or writ before the High Court can be moved to stay arrest directives.
