DELHI HIGH COURT LITIGATION FAQS

Essential statutory thresholds, roster conventions, and procedural requirements for litigants approaching the High Court of Delhi.

What is the current pecuniary jurisdiction for filing an original civil suit in the Delhi High Court?

expand_more
Under the Delhi High Court (Amendment) Act, 2015, the Delhi High Court exercises original pecuniary jurisdiction in ordinary civil suits exceeding ₹2.00 Crores (Two Crore Rupees). Suits valued at ₹2.00 Crores or less must be instituted before the competent Principal District & Sessions Courts across Delhi's 11 judicial districts. For Commercial Suits filed under the Commercial Courts Act, 2015, specified value thresholds are coordinated accordingly.

How does urgent mentioning for same-day listing work at the Delhi High Court?

expand_more
Urgent matters requiring immediate same-day consideration (such as imminent demolition, detention, auction, or arrest) require an urgent mentioning slip submitted to the Hon’ble Chief Justice Court / Senior Vacation Judge precisely between 10:30 AM and 11:00 AM. Once oral permission is granted upon prima facie justification of urgency, the Registry assigns a matter number and directs the bench clerk for physical or supplementary inclusion.

Can an Anticipatory Bail application be filed directly in the Delhi High Court without approaching the Sessions Court first?

expand_more
While Section 438 of the CrPC / Section 482 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) provides concurrent jurisdiction to both the Sessions Court and the High Court, standard practice requires filing before the Sessions Court first. However, direct High Court filing is permitted under exceptional circumstances, such as political vendetta, imminent multi-state arrest by specialized central agencies (CBI, ED, NIA), or severe inter-state jurisdictional questions.

What are the strict requirements for challenging a commercial arbitration award under Section 34 at Delhi High Court?

expand_more
Under Section 34 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996, an application must be instituted within a non-negotiable limitation period of 3 months (extendable by maximum 30 days only upon strict proof of sufficient cause). Challenges are restricted to narrow statutory grounds: patent illegality, conflict with the fundamental public policy of India, or violation of principles of natural justice without merits re-adjudication.

Does the Delhi High Court permit hybrid or virtual hearings for outstation clients?

expand_more
Yes. The Delhi High Court operates fully functional hybrid courtrooms across all benches pursuant to the High Court of Delhi Video Conferencing Rules, 2021. Litigants and external advocates can appear virtually through the official Cisco Webex platform with prior registry intimation and adhering to established judicial courtroom dress and decorum standards.
