Anticipatory bail is a pre-arrest legal directive issued by a Sessions Court or the High Court directing that an individual be released on bail immediately in the event of arrest for a non-bailable offense. Governed by Section 438 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 and Section 482 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS), an application for anticipatory bail can be filed when an individual has a reasonable apprehension of arrest arising from an FIR or criminal complaint.

This article details the legal mechanics of anticipatory bail in Delhi, judicial evaluation criteria, mandatory conditions, and step-by-step court procedures. ## Statutory Authority for Pre-Arrest Protection

Anticipatory bail protects individuals against malicious prosecution, personal humiliation, and custodial detention. The statutory authority is derived from:

- **Section 438 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (CrPC)** (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number).
- **Section 482 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)** (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number).

Unlike regular bail under Section 439 CrPC / BNSS Section 483 (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number), which is sought after an individual is taken into police custody, anticipatory bail is sought prior to arrest while the applicant remains free.

## Judicial Evaluation Criteria for Granting Pre-Arrest Bail

Courts do not grant anticipatory bail as a matter of routine. In landmark judgments such as *Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia v. State of Punjab (1980)* and *Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi) (2020)* (VERIFY: advocate to confirm citations), constitutional benches of the Supreme Court established key factors evaluated by judges:

1. **Nature and Gravity of Accusation:** The severity of the alleged offense, potential punishment, and whether custodial interrogation is essential for investigation.
2. **Antecedents of Applicant:** The criminal history, prior convictions, and general standing of the applicant in society.
3. **Flight Risk:** The likelihood of the applicant absconding or evading trial processes.
4. **Risk of Tampering:** The potential for the applicant to influence witnesses, alter documentary evidence, or impede police investigation.
5. **Malafide Intent:** Whether accusations stem from personal enmity, commercial disputes, or matrimonial discord intended to humiliate the applicant.

## Step-by-Step Anticipatory Bail Procedure in Delhi

Filing an anticipatory bail application in Delhi involves a structured court process:

```
[Apprehension of Arrest / FIR Registration]
                   │
                   ▼
[Drafting Anticipatory Bail Petition & Supporting Affidavit]
                   │
                   ▼
[Filing before Sessions Court (District Court Complex) or Delhi High Court]
                   │
                   ▼
[Urgent Mentioning & Listing before Roster Bench]
                   │
                   ▼
[Initial Hearing: Notice to State / IO & Interim Protection Review]
                   │
                   ▼
[Submission of Status Report by Investigating Officer (IO)]
                   │
                   ▼
[Final Arguments & Order Granting Pre-Arrest Bail with Conditions]
```

### Stage 1: Sessions Court Filing
Ordinarily, the application is first instituted before the Court of Sessions at the relevant District Court complex (Tis Hazari, Patiala House, Saket, Rohini, Karkardooma, or Dwarka) having territorial jurisdiction over the police station.

### Stage 2: Urgent Listing & Interim Protection
Upon filing, counsel mentions the matter before the Principal District & Sessions Judge or designated Special Judge. In urgent situations where arrest is imminent, the court may grant ex-parte **interim protection** restraining police arrest until the Investigating Officer (IO) files a formal Status Report.

### Stage 3: High Court Petition
If the Sessions Court rejects the anticipatory bail application, an immediate petition is filed before the High Court of Delhi under Section 482 BNSS / Section 438 CrPC (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number).

## Standard Statutory Conditions Imposed on Pre-Arrest Bail

When granting anticipatory bail, courts impose mandatory conditions under Section 438(2) CrPC / BNSS Section 482(2) (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number) to ensure cooperation with law enforcement:

- **Cooperation with Investigation:** The applicant must make themselves available for interrogation by the investigating officer whenever required.
- **No Witness Inducement:** The applicant shall not directly or indirectly make any inducement, threat, or promise to any witness or person acquainted with the facts of the case.
- **Travel Restrictions:** The applicant shall not leave India without prior permission of the court and must surrender their passport if directed.
- **Furnishing Bail Bonds:** Upon formal arrest, the police officer must release the applicant upon execution of personal bonds and local surety bonds to the satisfaction of the IO or court.
