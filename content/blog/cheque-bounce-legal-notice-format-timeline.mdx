A cheque bounce legal notice is a formal statutory demand letter sent by a payee to a drawer within 30 days of receiving a bank dishonour memo under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881. The notice mandates that the drawer pay the exact dishonoured cheque amount within 15 days of receipt. If the drawer fails to pay within this statutory cure window, a criminal complaint can be filed in court within 30 days. Proper drafting and proof of delivery are essential to maintain a valid cause of action.

This article provides an exhaustive practical analysis of legal notice requirements, statutory timelines, postal dispatch rules, essential clauses, and defense considerations under Delhi jurisdiction. ## Statutory Timelines for Sending a Cheque Bounce Legal Notice

Timing is critical under Chapter XVII of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number). Missing a single statutory deadline can render a criminal complaint non-maintainable.

1. **Cheque Presentation:** Present the cheque to the bank within its 3-month validity period from the date written on the instrument.
2. **Receipt of Return Memo:** Receive the official memo from the bank detailing the dishonour reason (e.g., Code 01 - Funds Insufficient).
3. **Dispatch of Legal Notice:** Issue and send the legal notice within **30 days** of receiving the bank memo under Section 138 proviso (b) (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number).
4. **15-Day Cure Window:** Allow the drawer **15 full days** from the date of notice delivery to make payment under Section 138 proviso (c) (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number).
5. **Filing of Criminal Complaint:** File the criminal complaint before the Metropolitan Magistrate within **30 days** from the 16th day after notice receipt under Section 142(1)(b) NI Act (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number).

## Essential Elements of a Section 138 Legal Notice

A legally resilient cheque bounce notice must contain clear factual recitals and precise statutory demands. Key elements include:

- **Identification of Parties:** Full names, addresses, and official designations of payee and drawer.
- **Transaction History:** Background details of the transaction or commercial agreement giving rise to the legally enforceable debt.
- **Cheque Details:** Specific cheque number, date, drawn bank, branch, and exact amount.
- **Presentation & Dishour Details:** Date of bank presentation, date of dishonour memo, and bank return reason.
- **Unconditional Demand for Payment:** Explicit demand for payment of the exact cheque amount within 15 days of notice receipt.
- **Warning of Criminal Consequences:** Notice that failure to pay will result in criminal prosecution under Section 138 NI Act, entailing up to 2 years imprisonment and fines up to double the cheque value.

## Proof of Service: Speed Post & RPAD Rules

Establishing valid delivery of the legal notice is a common evidentiary challenge in court. The payee must prove that the notice reached the drawer or was properly dispatched to their correct address.

### Presumption of Valid Service

Under Section 27 of the General Clauses Act, 1897 and Section 114 of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872 (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section numbers), service of notice by registered post or speed post is legally presumed when:
1. The notice is correctly addressed to the drawer's known residential or business location.
2. Postage is fully prepaid.
3. The notice is handed over to the postal authority under a registered consignment receipt.

As held by the Supreme Court of India in *C.C. Alavi Haji v. Palapetty Muhammed (2007)* (VERIFY: advocate to confirm citation), if a drawer claims non-receipt of notice, they must pay the cheque amount within 15 days of receiving the court summons. If they fail to do so, they cannot claim non-service as a defense to quash proceedings.

## How to Reply to a Cheque Bounce Legal Notice

If you receive a Section 138 legal notice, responding within 15 days is critical to establish your defense early and prevent ex-parte criminal summons.

Common defense grounds in notice replies include:
- **Security Cheque Defense:** Establishing that the cheque was issued as a security for an unexecuted contract, not for an existing debt.
- **Lack of Legally Enforceable Debt:** Demonstrating that the underlying obligation was non-existent, illegal, or satisfied.
- **Time-Barred Debt:** Showing that the cheque was drawn for a debt barred by limitation under the Limitation Act, 1963 (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number).
- **Material Alteration:** Proving unauthorized modifications to the cheque date, payee name, or amount without drawer consent under Section 87 NI Act (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number).
