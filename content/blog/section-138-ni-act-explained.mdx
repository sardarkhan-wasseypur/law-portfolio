Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 is a statutory provision in Indian law that penalizes the dishonour of a cheque issued for the discharge of a legally enforceable debt or monetary liability. If a cheque is returned unpaid by the bank due to insufficient funds, account closure, or stop-payment instructions, the payee can initiate criminal proceedings after sending a statutory 30-day legal notice. If the drawer fails to make full payment within 15 days of receiving the notice, a criminal complaint can be instituted before the Metropolitan Magistrate within 30 days.

This guide provides a detailed analysis of Section 138 NI Act proceedings for payees and drawers in Delhi courts, covering statutory pre-requisites, court jurisdiction, evidentiary presumptions, and defense strategies. ## Statutory Pre-Requisites for Filing a Section 138 Complaint

To successfully institute a criminal complaint under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number), five indispensable statutory conditions must be satisfied sequentially:

1. **Active Bank Account & Authorization:** The cheque must be drawn on a bank account maintained by the drawer for the discharge of a pre-existing legally enforceable debt or liability.
2. **Presentation within Validity Period:** The cheque must be presented to the bank within three months from its date of issuance (or within its period of validity).
3. **Official Bank Dishour Memo:** The cheque must be returned unpaid by the drawee bank accompanied by an official return memo specifying reasons such as "Funds Insufficient" (Code 01), "Exceeds Arrangement" (Code 02), or "Account Closed" (Code 05).
4. **Mandatory 30-Day Legal Notice:** The payee must issue a written legal demand notice to the drawer within 30 days of receiving the bank dishonour memo.
5. **15-Day Cure Window Expiry:** The drawer must fail to make payment of the cheque amount within 15 days of receiving the statutory notice. The cause of action arises on the 16th day.

## The 30-Day Legal Notice and 15-Day Cure Window

The statutory notice is the jurisdictional cornerstone of a Section 138 complaint. A defect in drafting or service of the notice can result in dismissal of the complaint.

The notice must be delivered via Registered Post AD or Speed Post to the correct address of the drawer. Under Section 27 of the General Clauses Act, 1897 (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number), service is legally presumed when the notice is correctly addressed and posted.

Key timelines to observe:
- **Notice Period:** 30 days from receipt of the bank return memo.
- **Cure Period:** 15 days from receipt of the notice by the drawer.
- **Limitation for Filing Complaint:** 30 days from the expiry of the 15-day cure window.

## Evidentiary Presumptions under Section 118 and Section 139

When a cheque bounce case reaches trial, the law provides two powerful evidentiary presumptions in favor of the payee under Section 118(a) and Section 139 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number):

- **Presumption of Consideration (Section 118a):** The court presumes that every negotiable instrument was made or drawn for consideration.
- **Presumption of Legally Enforceable Debt (Section 139):** The court presumes that the holder of a cheque received it for the discharge of a whole or partial debt or liability.

### Rebutting the Reverse Burden of Proof

These statutory presumptions shift the initial burden of proof onto the accused drawer. However, as established by the Supreme Court of India in *Rangappa v. Sri Mohan (2010)* (VERIFY: advocate to confirm citation), the accused does not need to prove their defense beyond a reasonable doubt. The accused can rebut the presumption on a **preponderance of probabilities** by establishing:

- That the cheque was handed over as an advance or security cheque for an unfulfilled transaction.
- That the underlying debt was time-barred under the Limitation Act, 1963 (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number).
- That the payee lacked financial capacity to advance the alleged cash loan.

## Section 138 Court Procedure in Delhi District Courts

Cheque bounce cases in Delhi are adjudicated by Metropolitan Magistrates in specialized NI Act Courts across Judicial Districts (Tis Hazari, Patiala House, Saket, Rohini, Karkardooma, and Dwarka).

The procedural stages include:

1. **Filing & Cognizance:** Submitting the complaint accompanied by the original cheque, return memo, postal receipt, tracking report, legal notice, and evidence affidavit under Section 145 NI Act (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number).
2. **Pre-Summoning Evidence:** Recording the complainant's statement and issuing summons to the accused under Section 204 CrPC / BNSS Section 227 (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number).
3. **Framing of Notice:** Framing formal charges/notice under Section 251 CrPC (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number) upon the appearance of the accused.
4. **Defense Evidence & Cross-Examination:** Cross-examination of the complainant and examination of defense witnesses.
5. **Final Arguments & Judgment:** Adjudication by the court. Upon conviction, the court may award imprisonment up to two years and fine up to double the cheque amount.

If criminal proceedings stem from commercial or police disputes, accused individuals may also explore remedies under [Anticipatory bail in Delhi](/legal-insights/anticipatory-bail-delhi-how-it-works/).

## Compounding of Offence under Section 147 NI Act

Section 147 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 (VERIFY: advocate to confirm section number) makes cheque bounce offences explicitly compoundable. Payee and drawer can enter into a mutual settlement at any stage of trial or appellate review.

Under the Supreme Court guidelines in *Damodar S. Prabhu v. Sayed Babalal H. (2010)* (VERIFY: advocate to confirm citation), compounding costs increase progressively based on the stage of trial:
- **Magistrate Court Stage:** 0% additional cost.
- **Sessions Court Appeal:** 10% of the cheque amount as costs to DSLSA.
- **High Court / Supreme Court Revision:** 15% of the cheque amount as costs.
